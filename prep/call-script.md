# Call script: Kelvin, Coach Hub (30–45 min)

**Your role on this call is not "the techie".** You are the business owner running the
project. Kelvin is the technical one, and he knows it. Your job is to show four things:
1. We took his work seriously and found real things.
2. We have a careful way of working (important because this app pays people).
3. We listen: get his answers to the pay questions.
4. A clear next step and the IT partner deal: build ~~RM12,000~~ → **RM5,000**, plus
   RM1,500/month, plus RM25 per new member.

The written report does the technical talking. You don't have to explain code. You only
have to walk through a document he can read too.

Have open on screen: `audit-report-2026-11-14.md` (share it) and `invoice-mockup.html`.
Have beside you: `call-briefing.md` (cheat sheet) and a notepad.

---

## 1. Open (2 min)

> "Thanks for the time, Kelvin. And honestly, well done on Coach Hub. You built a lot in a
> short time, and the plan you wrote is really clear. That's why we could test it properly."

Then set the agenda:

> "I'll take you through three things: what we checked and found, a few questions on how
> pay should work, and then what we'd propose and the cost. Sound good?"

## 2. What we found (10–15 min)

Share the report. **Start with "What is already solid"**, read a few lines out. Then:

> "So the base is good. Before coaches get paid from it, we found 7 things to tighten.
> None of them show up when you click through the app. They're in the rules underneath."

Go through the table in your own words. You only need the plain-English version:

| # | Say it like this |
|---|---|
| 1 | "A manager can see private info, like phone, address and notes, about people outside their studio. Even yours." |
| 2 | "Coaches can see the studio pay rates if they know where to look." |
| 3 | "The QR check-in can be done without being at the studio." |
| 4 | "Once a class is verified, it can still be changed. That would change pay later." |
| 5 | "A manager can put a coach into a Lead spot who isn't approved for Lead." |
| 6 | "Challenge shares can add up to more than 100% of the pool." |
| 7 | "There's no test copy and no backup. Every change goes straight to your coaches." |

Then the key line:

> "None of this is unusual for a first version. It's the kind of thing that only shows up
> when someone deliberately tries to break it. That's what we do."

## 3. How we work (3 min)

This is your real selling point. Say it honestly. He uses AI too, so don't pretend otherwise:

> "We build with AI too, same as you. The difference is the process around it. One AI
> writes the change, and a separate one's only job is to try to break it. Every pay change
> gets checked against a month we've worked out by hand, to the cent, before it goes live.
> For an app that pays people, that checking is what you're paying for."

Show the mock-up and the hand-worked invoice:

> "This is one coach's October, worked out by hand from your rules. RM2,805.33. Once you
> confirm the rates, that becomes the test every pay change has to pass."

## 4. Pay questions: let HIM talk (10 min)

> "Writing that invoice raised some questions your plan doesn't answer yet. Better to
> settle them now than after it's built."

Ask these and **write down his answers**. Don't debate them. It's his business, his call.

1. If a coach has a personal rate of RM65 and a special event pays RM80, which do they get?
2. When you choose "deduction" for a late coach, is it a fixed RM amount or a %?
3. When does the month close, and when do coaches get paid?
4. Is exactly 10 minutes late counted as late?
5. If RM2,500 splits between 3 coaches, there's 1 sen left over. Who gets it?
6. If you cancel a class last minute, does the coach get anything?
7. Full-time coaches who do PT or challenges: does that go on an invoice or on payroll?
8. If a coach disputes their invoice, who sorts it out?
9. Is a coach's personal rate one rate, or separate Lead and Support rates?

Also ask:
- "Are you still working on the code since September, or is what's on GitHub the latest?"
- "What rates do you pay today, Lead and Support?"
- "Which bank do you pay coaches from?"

If he doesn't know some answers: "No problem, send them when you have them."

## 5. Proposal: the IT partner deal (5–8 min)

**Step 1: show the full job and its normal price first.** Say it slowly. This number makes
the offer feel generous.

> "To finish Coach Hub properly, here's everything involved:"
>
> 1. Fix the 13 issues, each with a test, plus a test copy and backups
> 2. Finish testing Stage 2: claiming, QR check-in, verification, on a real phone
> 3. Build Stage 3 pay: rates, invoices, PDF, bank details, bank file
> 4. Go live, and support a 2–3 week pilot with your coaches
>
> "Quoted as a normal project, that's **RM12,000**."

**Step 2: the offer.**

> "But we don't want to be just a supplier. We'd like to be your IT partner, so we win
> when you win. So here's what we'd propose instead:"

| | Normal project | Our partner deal |
|---|---|---|
| Coach Hub build (all 4 steps) | ~~RM12,000~~ | **RM5,000** |
| Monthly: Coach Hub support, Hiiiilo, all your IT help | separate | **RM1,500 / month** |
| Bonus | — | **RM25 per new member** who joins any of your studios |

> "So you save RM7,000 up front. The monthly covers all your IT in one place, Hiiiilo
> included. And we only earn more when you sign up new members. Quiet month, you just pay
> the base. Great month, like the Subang launch, we share the win."

**Step 3: make it concrete with Subang.**

> "Say Subang opens with 200 new members. That's a RM5,000 bonus to us, and 200 members'
> worth of fees to you. Our bonus is a small share of that, only when it happens."

Terms to say (and put in writing afterwards):

- **12 months**, review together after 6.
- **"New member"** = a new paying membership in the month, from your Glofox report. Not
  trials or drop-ins.
- **Payment:** build 50% to start, 50% when you sign it off on your phone. Monthly + bonus
  invoiced at the start of each month for the previous month.
- **Your running costs** (hosting, about US$45/month) are paid by you directly. Glofox sync
  and WhatsApp reminders are priced separately, since they depend on BFT/ABC Fitness and
  Meta approving them.
- **You own the code** once the build is paid.

**Step 4: plant the future (one sentence, don't push):**

> "And if it works well, other BFT owners have exactly the same coach problem. Down the
> line we could offer it to them together, with a share coming back to you."

**Step 5: close.**

> "I'll send this in writing after the call, with the full report. If you're happy, we start
> with the fixes and the test copy this week."

**Agree one concrete next step**, with a date.

### If he pushes back on price

| He says | You say |
|---|---|
| "RM1,500 a month is a lot." | "It covers Coach Hub support and Hiiiilo together. Hiiiilo alone was RM599, and this is all your IT in one place. Cheaper than any part-time IT person." |
| "Why should I pay a bonus per member?" | "It's what lets us drop the build from RM12k to RM5k. We take some of the risk with you." |
| "Can the bonus be lower?" | Write it down. "Let me check with the team." Don't drop the price on the call. |
| "What if I only want the build?" | "Then it's the normal project price, RM12,000, plus monthly support if you want it." |

## When he asks something technical you can't answer

This **will** happen, and it's completely fine. Say one of these. Never guess.

> "Good question. Let me get you a proper answer in writing rather than half an answer now."

> "I'll check that with the team and come back to you by tomorrow."

Write the question down word for word. After the call, paste it to Claude in this project.
You'll get a plain answer you can send him.

Things he might ask, with an answer you can actually give:

| He asks | You say |
|---|---|
| "How did you test it?" | "On our own copy of your database with fake coaches. We signed in as each role and tried to see and change everything. We never touched your live site." |
| "Can't I just fix these myself with Claude?" | "You could, and the report tells you what. The value is the checking afterwards: proving each fix works and nothing else broke. That's the part that's easy to skip when you're busy running studios." |
| "Is my coaches' data at risk right now?" | "Only from your own staff, and only if they know how to dig. It's not open to the public. But it should be fixed before more people are added." |
| "Which tools do you use?" | "Claude, same as you, with a separate checker AI and tests on top." |
| "How long will it take?" | "Roughly a week for the fixes and test copy, then a few weeks for pay, then the 2–3 week pilot. I'll put exact dates in writing." |

## Don'ts

- Don't say his code is bad. Say "harden", "tighten", "before coaches are paid from it".
- Don't promise dates or prices you haven't decided. Say "in the written quote".
- Don't argue his business rules. Write them down.
- Don't pretend to know something. "I'll come back to you" builds more trust than a wrong answer.
