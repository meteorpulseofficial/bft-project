# Meeting with Kelvin: train + talk, BFT Bangsar (19 Nov)

Read on your phone before you go in.

## The big picture (calms the nerves)

- **Coach Hub is already sold.** He said yes to RM5,000 + RM25 per new member. Tomorrow is
  not a pitch. It's agreeing **how to start**.
- **He invited you to train.** That means he likes you. Be a friend first, business second.
- **You don't need to talk tech.** He knows the code. Your job is the plan, the people and
  the next step.

**Priority order:**
1. **Coach Hub:** lock in the start.
2. **Marketing:** the biggest money (~RM7k/month). Talk about it, don't push it.
3. **Hiiiilo:** don't bring it up. Only if he does.

---

## Part 1: The workout (this is marketing research)

Train like a brand-new member, because that's exactly who his marketing is aimed at.

- Notice what confuses you or surprises you: your name on the screen, the heart-rate
  colours, the PXI medals, the station videos.
- Ask him questions like a curious prospect: *"What does gold mean?" "How do people know
  what to do at each station?"*
- Afterwards those become your best marketing line: **"I felt what your new members feel."**
- Ask before taking any photos or videos.

## Part 2: The chat after

### Open (casual)

> "That was tough! I get it now. The screen with your name on it, the medals, it's really
> different. No wonder you want marketing that explains it."

### Coach Hub: leave with these 3 things

> "So on Coach Hub, we're ready to start. Just want to agree how we kick off."

1. **Start date + deposit:** *"Can we start this week? We usually do 50% to start, 50% when
   you've tested it on your phone and are happy."*
2. **Monthly support:** still open, because the RM1,500 included Hiiiilo, which is on
   hold. Suggest: *"For ongoing support after launch, fixes, small changes, help on payroll
   day, we'd suggest RM500 a month."* If he hesitates: *"No problem, let's agree that before
   go-live."*
3. **Three pay questions** (write his answers down):
   - "If a coach has a personal rate and a special event pays more, which one do they get?"
   - "When does the month close, and when do coaches get paid?"
   - "What rates do you pay today, Lead and Support?"

   Say: *"I've got a few more small ones. I'll WhatsApp them so you can answer in your own
   time."*

Also ask: *"Is what's on GitHub the latest version, or have you changed things since
September?"*

**If he asks anything technical:**
> "Good question. I'll get you a proper answer in writing tonight."

Write it down. Send it to Claude later.

### Marketing: ask, don't sell

> "How's the agency search going? Did you get a chance to look at our proposal?"

Then listen. Find out:
- **Who else** he's talking to, and what he liked about them
- **What matters most** to him: price, lead quality, the content style?
- **When he'll decide** (his current agency leaves end of December)

Things you can say honestly:
- *"We're already inside your business through Coach Hub. We know your coaches, studios
  and growth plans. A new agency starts from zero."*
- *"After today's class, I know what to film first: the moment your name pops up on the
  screen."*
- *"We'd measure success by members joining, not by likes. That's why we offered the bonus
  per member."*

If he says the price is high: *"The packages are adjustable. Which parts matter most to
you? I'll rework it."* **Don't cut the price on the spot.**

### Hiiiilo: only if he brings it up

If he mentions leads piling up, his sales team being busy, or AI chatbots:
> "That's exactly what Hiiiilo is for, but no rush. Let's get the marketing right first.
> Once more leads come in than your team can handle, that's the time to add it."

Otherwise: **say nothing.** He said he's not sure. Pushing it now could make him feel sold
to and cost you the bigger deals.

### Close

> "So next step: I'll send you the Coach Hub agreement and deposit invoice by [day], plus
> the remaining pay questions. And whenever you're ready, I'm happy to walk through the
> marketing proposal properly."

**Leave with one clear next step and a date.**

## Don'ts

- Don't call his code bad. Say "tighten" or "harden".
- Don't cut prices on the spot. Say "let me rework it".
- Don't promise dates you haven't checked.
- Don't push Hiiiilo.
- Don't talk business mid-workout unless he starts it.
