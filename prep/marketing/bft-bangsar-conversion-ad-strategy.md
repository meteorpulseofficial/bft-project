# BFT Bangsar: conversion ad strategy (draft, 23 Nov 2026)

**Goal.** Fewer leads, better leads: people who already accept a premium price before they
message us. Sales can handle 6–7 leads a day, and only about 10% of today's leads join.

**The price problem in one line.** BFT is RM130 a week (about RM520 a month). People compare it
with a RM180 budget gym. Our job is to move the comparison to what BFT actually replaces:
**boutique classes and personal training.**

Studio: Bangsar first. Audience from the studio: mostly 30–50, a mix of business owners and
employees.

---

## 1. The price, made to feel worth it

| What they compare with | Typical KL price | BFT at 4 classes a week | BFT at 5 a week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget gym (Anytime) | ~RM180/month, but most people go 1–2× a week | RM32.50 a class | RM26 a class |
| Boutique class (F45 and similar) | RM40–90 a class [1] | cheaper per class | cheaper per class |
| Personal trainer | RM105 (chain gym) to RM250–400 (Bangsar boutique) a session [2][3] | a fraction of PT | a fraction of PT |

**The line to use:** *"Coached, programmed and tracked like a PT session, at a price per class
lower than most boutique studios."*

Other price reducers:
- **No lock-in contract.** They can stop any week. Removes the "what if I don't use it" fear.
- **Unlimited classes, all day.** The more they come, the cheaper each class gets.
- **One membership, ~330 studios worldwide.** Business trips to Singapore or Australia don't
  break the habit.

Rule: **never hide the price in the ad.** Showing "from RM130/week" filters out deal-hunters
before they cost us a sales call.

## 2. Who to target (ranked)

### A. The class-hopper (start here)
- **Who:** 30–45, already spending RM300–800 a month on classes, ClassPass, F45, spin or a PT.
- **Why first:** they already pay boutique prices, so BFT looks *cheaper*, not dearer.
- **What they want:** variety without planning, a sense of progress, value for money.
- **Pain:** paying per class adds up; no record of progress; classes feel random.
- **Best angles:** cost per class (1), progress you can see (4), unlimited (5).

### B. The time-poor business owner
- **Who:** 35–50, runs a business, price is not the issue, time and focus are.
- **What they want:** a workout that is done for them: show up, follow the screen, leave.
- **Pain:** no time to plan; PT schedules clash with meetings; gym visits drift.
- **Best angles:** 45 minutes then back to work (7), PT structure at group price (6),
  same workout worldwide (8).

### C. The stalled employee
- **Who:** 30–45, manager level, pays for a gym they rarely use, feels guilty about it.
- **What they want:** structure and someone expecting them.
- **Pain:** wasted gym fee; no plan; no results; gets bored alone.
- **Best angles:** the gym you're paying for but not using (2), community (9), no guessing (3).

### D. The 40-plus comeback
- **Who:** 40–50, often a parent, restarting after years off, worried about injury and looking
  unfit next to others.
- **What they want:** safe guidance, their own pace, a welcoming room.
- **Pain:** intimidation; "I'm not fit enough to start a class".
- **Best angles:** train to *your* heart rate (10), video demo at every station (3),
  buddy system (9).
- **Use for:** a slower, warmer funnel (more proof, more class walk-throughs before the offer).

### Who not to chase
Under-25s and students, people who only react to discounts, people outside ~7 km of Telawi.
Every one of these costs a sales call and rarely joins.

## 2a. Target audience, the paid-ads way (who, ranked, and how to reach them)

**The core buyer, in one sentence:** a Bangsar-area professional aged 32–50, earning well, who
already spends money on their health but isn't getting the results, the structure or the time
efficiency they want — and who would rather pay more for something that works than less for
something they don't use.

That person splits into five segments. On Meta we **don't** target these with interests: we
target one broad audience and let a different ad speak to each segment (the creative does the
targeting).

### Ranked segments

| Rank | Segment | Age | Why they're worth it | Main pain | What they want | Identity words for hooks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Boutique spenders** — already pay for pilates, spin, F45-style classes, a PT or ClassPass | 32–45 | Already pay RM300–800/month, so BFT looks *cheaper*; used to classes | Money spent, no proof of progress, random workouts | Progress they can see, one place, better value per class | pilates regulars, spin regulars, ClassPass users, runners |
| **2** | **Business owners** | 38–50 | Highest income; price isn't the issue; stay long if it fits their week | No time, no plan, health slipping while the business grows | Done-for-you workout, efficient, accountable | founders, business owners, directors |
| **3** | **Busy parents** (mostly mums; dads too) | 35–48 | Train in school hours and early mornings when classes have space; very loyal once in | No time for themselves, energy, getting back in shape after kids | 50 minutes that fit between drop-off and work, a friendly room | Bangsar mums, working mums, dads |
| **4** | **Corporate professionals** with a gym they don't use | 30–40 | Biggest pool; respond to structure and community | Guilt, wasted fee, no plan, boredom | Someone expecting them, a plan, people to train with | lawyers, bankers, consultants, doctors |
| **5** | **Comeback 45+** | 45–55 | High income, loyal; but slower to decide | Fear of injury and of being the least fit | Safe start, own pace, coaches watching | "people in their 40s", "starting again" |

**Plus one cross-cutting trigger: expats and frequent travellers.** Many Bangsar/Mont Kiara
professionals are both. "One membership, ~330 studios worldwide" works as a line inside any
segment's ad.

**Who we pay *not* to reach:** under-28s, students, people who only respond to discounts, people
outside the radius. The price line in every ad filters them out.

### Full audience profile (the identifiers that go into the creative)

- **Where they live and work:** Bangsar, Bangsar South, Bukit Damansara, Damansara Heights, Mont
  Kiara, TTDI, Mid Valley, KL Sentral offices.
- **Daily life:** early starts, Grab and traffic, back-to-back meetings, school runs, weekend
  brunch on Telawi, Sunday runs or walks at Bukit Kiara.
- **What they've tried:** budget gym membership, PT packages, pilates/spin packs, ClassPass, home
  workouts, running apps, a smartwatch they check daily.
- **Why it didn't stick:** no plan, no one checking on them, boredom, cost per class adding up,
  classes full at the times they can go, didn't see progress.
- **Fears:** wasting more money, getting injured, looking unfit in a group, health catching up
  with them in their 40s.
- **Hopes:** more energy, fitting into work clothes, keeping up with their kids, feeling in control.
- **Words they use** (to confirm with real members): "no time", "I need structure", "I get bored",
  "I need someone to push me", "I want to see results", "it's too expensive to go only twice a week".
- **Objections:** "RM520 a month?", "I'm not fit enough yet", "I already do pilates/running",
  "group classes aren't for me", "what if I stop going?".

### How to set it up on Meta

| Setting | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Location | ~7–8 km around Jalan Telawi (pin), "people living in or recently in" | covers Bangsar, Mont Kiara, TTDI, Damansara, Mid Valley |
| Age | 28–55, all genders | wide enough for Meta to learn; creative narrows it |
| Interests | **none** | interest stacking hurts on Meta now; the ad's words do the targeting |
| Ads | one ad per segment (identity word in the first line) | each segment gets an ad that names them |
| Exclude | current members (customer list from Glofox), leads from the last 30 days | don't pay to reach people already in |
| Lookalike test | 1% lookalike of members who stayed 3+ months, as a second ad set | the best members, not all members |
| Budget split | ~70% on the broad set, ~30% on tests (new segments, lookalike) | playbook's testing split |

### What would sharpen this (ask Kelvin)

A Glofox export of current members — **age, postcode, join date, still active or not, how they
heard of us** — tells us which segment really stays longest. Rank 1–5 above is our best judgement
until then; the first 3 weeks of ads will confirm or reorder it.

## 4. Funnel

| Stage | Who sees it | What they see | Share of budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Broad 30–50, ~7 km around Telawi, Bangsar/Bukit Damansara/Damansara Heights/Mont Kiara/TTDI/Mid Valley | 15–30 s education videos: angles 11, 3, 4, 13 | ~50% |
| **Warm** | Watched 50%+ of a video, engaged with IG/FB, visited the website | Proof: member stories (12), price maths (1), "what happens in your first class" | ~30% |
| **Ready** | Watched several videos, visited pricing | The offer + WhatsApp: "Memberships from RM130/week. Book your first class." | ~20% |

**Lead capture: WhatsApp click-to-chat**, with a pre-filled message that names the ad
(e.g. "Hi, I saw the 45-minute video") so every lead is traced to its angle.
First reply asks three quick questions: goal, area, best time. Price is already known from the ad.

**Offer.** Keep the two-week trial, but never as the headline. Lead with the system, mention the
trial last. Test full-price trial (RM130) against 50% off: if full price brings fewer but better
leads, that's a win given the sales cap.

## 5. Test plan (first 6 weeks)

**Weeks 1–3: find the winning person and angle.**
- 8 ads: avatars A, B, C, D × 2 angles each. Same budget, same targeting, same offer.
- Judge on **cost per qualified lead** (in area, knows the price, books a trial), not cost per
  lead.

**Weeks 4–6: scale and refresh.**
- Move budget to the top 2–3 ads. Make 3 new hooks for each winning angle.
- Start the warm and ready stages using viewers from weeks 1–3.

**What to measure each week**
| Measure | Why |
|---|---|
| Cost per qualified lead | the real cost of a lead worth calling |
| Trial booked % | does the ad attract people who act |
| Trial show-up % | are they serious |
| **Trial → member %** | the number that matters most |
| Cost per new member | what a member really costs to win |
| Leads per day vs 6–7 cap | never flood sales |

If leads go over the sales cap, raise the filter (stronger price line, smaller radius) rather
than adding staff.

## 6a. Hook bank v2 (stronger: pain, stakes, identity)

**Why v1 was weak:** it described BFT (screens, medals, 45 minutes). A hook should name something
the viewer already feels — guilt, fear, wasted money, pride — before BFT appears at all.
v2 rules:
- Lead with **their** problem, not our feature. The product shows up after second 3.
- Be **specific**: a number, a time, a place, a moment ("12:15", "RM8,000", "at the park").
- Raise **stakes** for 30–50: health, energy for the kids, the business depending on you.
- Speak to **identity** ("business owners", "if you've done classes for years").
- **Meta rule:** ads can't say or imply *the viewer* has a health or body condition ("Is your
  cholesterol high?", "Your belly…" get rejected). Body and health hooks are written in the
  first person, spoken by a real member or coach. Every first-person line must be true for
  the person saying it.

### A. The class-hopper (lever: money spent, no proof of progress)
1. "You've done classes for two years. Can you prove you're fitter?"
2. "I spent RM8,000 on classes last year. My body didn't change."
3. "Sweating isn't progress. Most classes can't tell the difference."
4. "Your instructor has 30 people and no idea if you're improving."
5. "The day a screen showed me I'd been coasting for months."
6. "If your workout doesn't have a number, it's a guess."
7. "300 calories or 700? Your last class has no idea."
8. "I was paying boutique prices for random workouts."

#### A, more (priority target — five sub-angles to test)

**Money that went nowhere**
9. "Add up every class pack you bought this year. Now look in the mirror."
10. "RM50 a class feels small. RM7,000 a year doesn't."
11. "I was a 'regular' at three studios. I couldn't tell you if I got fitter at any of them."
12. "Expired class credits are the most expensive workout you never did."

**Hard is not the same as working**
13. "You leave every class drenched. So why do you look the same as last year?"
14. "Feeling destroyed after a class isn't proof it worked."
15. "Same class, same moves, same body. That's not training, that's a routine."
16. "If you can't say what you improved this month, you didn't train. You exercised."

**Nobody is tracking you**
17. "Your instructor knows your name. Not your heart rate, not your progress."
18. "Thirty people in the room, one instructor. Who's watching your numbers?"
19. "You'd never pay a trainer who doesn't track you. Why pay a class that doesn't?"
20. "Every workout I've done for two years, lost. Nothing recorded."

**Random workouts, random results**
21. "Spin Monday, pilates Wednesday, HIIT Friday. What's the plan?"
22. "Your workouts are chosen by whatever has a free slot."
23. "Variety is fun. But who's making sure it adds up?"
24. "I booked whatever class had space. My results showed it."

**The switch (the price reframe)**
25. "I left boutique classes and now pay less per class. And I finally see progress."
26. "Unlimited classes for less than my old class packs. I'm annoyed I waited."
27. "Same sweat as your favourite class, plus a score that proves it's working."
28. "No more booking wars at 7am. Classes run all day."

*Lines 11, 20, 24, 25, 26: first person, so only from a member for whom it's true.*

#### A, v3: in their world (scenes and words a 30–50 Bangsar class-goer would recognise)

Why v1–v2 missed: "class-hopper" was one generic person talking about money and progress.
In Bangsar this group is really four tribes, each with its own habits and words. Speak to one
tribe per ad. Facts used: BFT classes have 2–3 coaches per class and members come 4–5 times a
week on average (BFT figures — confirm for Bangsar); boutique studios here charge about RM45–100
a class. Don't name competitor studios in ads.

**Reformer and mat pilates regulars (mostly women 30–45)**
1. "Pilates made me stronger. It didn't make me fitter."
2. "I love my reformer class. I don't love paying RM90 for 50 minutes of it."
3. "Core: strong. Stairs to the office: still out of breath."
4. "Keep your pilates. Add the thing it doesn't do."
5. "My pilates instructor couldn't tell me my heart rate. Now I see it every class."

**Spin and HIIT studio regulars**
6. "Spin gave me cardio. My arms and back got nothing."
7. "Same playlist, same bike, same body for two years."
8. "I booked spin at 6am for the music. I stayed at BFT for the results."
9. "Dark room, loud music, no idea if I'm improving."
10. "HIIT three times a week, and still the same weight. Here's what was missing."

**Runners, Bukit Kiara regulars, race and Hyrox types (mostly men 30–50)**
11. "Running Kiara every Sunday doesn't fix your weak shoulders."
12. "Runners: when did you last train anything above the waist?"
13. "Training for a race? Your coach would want you doing this twice a week."
14. "I could run 10k but I couldn't do 10 push-ups."
15. "Strength and cardio in one hour, with your heart rate on the screen. Your watch will love it."

**ClassPass and class-pack collectors**
16. "Three studios, three apps, three booking windows. Every week."
17. "I spent more time booking classes than doing them."
18. "Credits expiring Sunday. Booked whatever was left. Sound familiar?"
19. "Every studio knows my name. None of them know my progress."
20. "One studio, one app, every class tracked. I deleted the other three."

**For the whole group (the switch)**
21. "Most studios give you one instructor for the room. BFT runs 2–3 coaches every class."
22. "Our members come 4–5 times a week. That's what happens when you stop paying per class."
23. "You already spend boutique money. You just don't get boutique tracking."
24. "Your class is good. It's just not a programme."
25. "What happens when a class has a plan, a screen and two coaches watching you."

### B. The business owner (lever: you are the business)
1. "If you get sick for three months, who runs your business?"
2. "Your business has a finance team. Your body has nobody."
3. "I was growing my company and losing my health. My doctor said it out loud at 42."
4. "You'd never run your business without numbers. Why run your body that way?"
5. "Every founder I know who's still going strong at 50 has one habit."
6. "I pay for results everywhere else. I was paying a gym for nothing."
7. "The only meeting of my day where someone else makes every decision."
8. "My team stopped seeing me exhausted at 3pm. They noticed before I did."

### C. The stalled employee (lever: guilt, wasted fee, no plan)
1. "You're not lazy. You just don't know what to do when you walk into a gym."
2. "Every January you sign up. Every March you stop. Here's what's different."
3. "Your colleagues think you go to the gym. You know the truth."
4. "Energy for Netflix but not the gym? That's not a motivation problem."
5. "Nobody at your gym would notice if you never came back. Here, they would."
6. "My work shirts stopped fitting. That was my wake-up call."
7. "Why 'I'll start Monday' never works, and what does."
8. "I paid for a gym for two years. I went maybe 20 times."

### D. The 40-plus comeback (lever: health, kids, ageing — first person only)
1. "My blood test came back 'borderline'. That was day one."
2. "I couldn't keep up with my son at the park. That hurt more than any workout."
3. "At 40, your body stops forgiving you. That's why this is the age to start."
4. "You don't need to get fit before you start. That's what the class is for."
5. "Everyone in this room was once the new person who couldn't finish."
6. "The fittest person in our 7am class is 52." *(only if true)*
7. "I was scared of getting injured. So I started at my own heart rate."
8. "I want to be the parent who still plays, not the one who watches."

### Filters (stop deal-hunters before they message)
1. "If you want the cheapest gym in Bangsar, scroll past."
2. "We're RM130 a week. Here's why people still choose us over a RM180 gym."
3. "This isn't for everyone. It's for people who are done guessing."
4. "Not a promo. Not a free trial gimmick. Just a better way to train."

**How to test:** pick the 2 strongest per group, shoot each with a real member or coach, and
let week 1–3 numbers decide. Better still, ask 10 members "what made you finally join?" — their
exact words usually beat anything written at a desk.

## 6b. Hooks built with the paid-ads playbook (Meta, 2026)

Three ideas from the playbook change how we write hooks:
1. **The creative does the targeting.** Target broadly (30–50, ~7 km around Telawi) and put the
   audience *in the words*. Meta then shows each ad to the people it describes.
2. **Identity-trigger keyword.** Keep one strong hook and swap in one word for who it's for
   ("for Bangsar **lawyers**", "for **runners**"). The viewer feels "that's me", and Meta learns who
   to show it to. One hook × 10 identities = 10 ads from one idea.
3. **Don't look like an ad.** Plain statics (notes-app text, a WhatsApp chat, a phone photo of the
   screen with names on it) are cheap to make and often beat polished video. Aim for volume:
   new statics every week.

Meta rule reminder: put age and health in the third person ("for people in their 40s"), never
"Are you over 40?" or "Is your back sore?".

### Base hooks × identity triggers

Identities for Bangsar 30–50: **lawyers, bankers, doctors, consultants, founders, business
owners, mums, dads, runners, pilates regulars, ClassPass users, expats, people in their 40s.**

| Base hook | Example swaps |
|---|---|
| "Why Bangsar **[lawyers]** are training 4 times a week here" | founders · mums · runners · doctors |
| "**[Bankers]** track every number at work. Here's the one they forget." | consultants · founders · business owners |
| "The 50-minute workout for **[founders]** who don't have time to plan workouts" | mums · dads · doctors on shift |
| "**[Pilates regulars]**: this is the half of fitness your class skips" | runners · spin regulars · yoga regulars |
| "If you're a **[ClassPass user]** in Bangsar, do this maths before you renew" | class-pack buyers · F45-style regulars |
| "Fitness for **[people in their 40s]** who don't want to feel like a beginner" | new parents · expats new to KL · people coming back after injury |
| "What **[a Mont Kiara mum]** does between school drop-off and her first meeting" | a Bangsar lawyer · a TTDI founder · a Damansara banker |

Swap one word at a time; keep everything else the same so results are comparable.

### Static ad ideas (native, low-cost)

### Retargeting: objection hooks (for people who watched but didn't message)
The playbook says retarget with a *different* message that answers why they didn't act:

| Objection | Hook |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | "RM130 a week. Here's what it costs per class if you come 4 times." |
| "I'm not fit enough" | "Every exercise has a video and your own heart rate target. Nobody's watching you fail." |
| "I don't have time" | "50 minutes, classes all day. Pick any slot that fits." |
| "I already do pilates/running" | "Keep it. Add the strength and cardio it doesn't cover." |
| "Group classes aren't for me" | "New members get a buddy on day one." |
| "What if I stop going?" | "No lock-in contract. Stop any week." |

Get the real objections by calling 10 leads who didn't join; use their words as headlines.
