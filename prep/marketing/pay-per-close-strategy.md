## 1. Short answer

**Your idea is good, and it is the right shape for Kelvin.** He wants to grow from ~140 to
300, then 400. A partner who only earns when members join and stay is easy for him to say
yes to, and it beats the RM7,000/month fixed fee for us once it gets going.

Make five changes so it doesn't backfire on us:

1. **Take a share of revenue, not profit.** Profit depends on his rent, coach pay and
   franchise fees. We can't see or check those, and he controls them. Revenue is one
   number from his Glofox report. Easy to check, nothing to argue about.
2. **Get paid every month the member stays (for 12 months), not once.** Then we earn more
   when members stay, so retention work pays us too. That's the "long-term partnership"
   you want, built into the price.
3. **Agree in writing what counts as "our" member** before we start (section 7).
4. **Kelvin keeps paying for the ads.** Ad money goes from his card to Meta, at least
   RM4,000/month. We earn for the work, not by funding his ads.
5. **Protect our early months.** Months 1–3 pay little whatever happens (chart below). A
   one-off setup fee, not a monthly fee, covers that gap (section 8).

**What 15% means in ringgit.** RM130 a week × 52 weeks = RM6,760 a year, about **RM563 a

For Kelvin that's about 1.3 months of membership to get a member. A normal cost for a

## 2a. The RM130 trial: don't charge on it

**Recommendation: charge nothing on the trial. Charge only once it becomes a membership.**

- **It's tiny money.** 15% of RM130 is RM19.50, about one week of a member's share.
- **It's a strong selling line.** "We don't earn a cent on trials, only on people who become
  members." That's exactly our pitch: judged on members, not on leads or freebie-seekers.
- **It keeps us honest.** If we earned on trials, it would look like we just want lots of
  cheap trial sign-ups, which is the problem he has today.
- **We don't lose the member.** A trial that came through our ads or messages and turns
  into a membership counts as ours. The 60-day window (section 7) starts at first contact,
  so a 2-week trial plus a few days to decide is easily covered.

The real number to win is **trial → member**. Ask Kelvin how many trial people join today.
That's the figure we promise to push up.

**What "no lock-in" means for us:** members can stop any week. So retention (section 6)
isn't a nice extra, it's our income. It also means we're paid weekly or fortnightly, as
each payment actually comes in.

## 4. The hidden problem: his sales team caps our income

His team can handle **6–7 leads a day**, and about **10% become members**.

> 6–7 leads × 30 days ≈ 200 leads → 10% ≈ **20 new members a month, at most.**

Even with perfect ads, we can't pass ~20 a month unless one of these changes:

1. **More leads become members.** That's the core of the proposal: leads who already
   understand the tech, PXI and the price. Going from 10% to 15% means 30 a month. It's the
   biggest lever we have.
2. **Win-backs** need little selling. Ex-members already know BFT.
3. **Retention.** Every member kept is one his team doesn't have to replace.
4. **A faster first reply.** Leads go cold in hours. Later, this is where Hiiiilo fits
   naturally, but don't push it (see the meeting guide).

Because we're paid when he closes, **we need a say in the follow-up**. Ask for: a reply to
every lead within 1 hour during opening hours, and a shared lead sheet so we can see what
happened to each lead.

**On his goal:** if about 5% of members cancel each month *(assumption)*, then at 300
members he loses ~15 a month just standing still. Getting to 300 needs better conversion
**and** better retention, not only more ads. Tell him this. It shows we understand his
business, not just ads.

## 5. Win-back plan: the old list ("where the money is")

You're right. People who already trained at BFT are the cheapest members to get back.
They know the workout, the coaches and the price. January (new year resolutions) is the
best month of the year to do it.

### Step 1: Gather and sort the list (December)

Export from Glofox and the sales team's WhatsApp/spreadsheets, then split into:

| Group | Who | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| A | Ex-members who left in the last 12 months | Highest: they remember the coaches |
| B | Trial or 2-week-offer people who never joined | High: they've been inside |
| C | Ex-members who left over a year ago | Medium |
| D | Leads who asked but never came in | Lower: treat like warm new leads |

Recent ex-members often come back at around 5–10% *(rough guide, not a promise)*. A list of
400 could mean 20–40 members back.

### Step 2: Stay legal and keep his WhatsApp number safe

- **Malaysia's data law (PDPA)** applies. Only message people who gave BFT their number and
  haven't asked to stop. Every message has a simple "reply STOP" option. BFT owns the list;
  we handle it under our agreement and don't keep a copy after.
- **Don't mass-blast from a normal phone.** WhatsApp bans numbers for that, and it could be
  his sales number. Use WhatsApp Business with approved message templates, in small
  batches. I'll set this up.
- The same list can be uploaded to Meta as a custom audience, to show "come back" ads only
  to these people (same consent rule).

### Step 3: The offer and the messages

- **Offer:** a "comeback" reason with an end date, e.g. a free week back, or the first
  month at a special rate, tied to the January 8-week challenge. Kelvin decides the offer.
- **Who sends it:** where possible, the coach they knew. "Hi Sarah, it's Coach Danny from
  BFT Bangsar. We miss you in class!" beats any brand message.
- **What's new since you left:** new programs, new coaches, the medals, the challenge.
- **Three touches:** first message → reminder after 3 days → "last chance" before the offer
  ends. Then stop.

Example (group A):

> Hi [name], Coach [name] from BFT Bangsar here 👋 It's been a while! We're starting an
> 8-week challenge on [date] and I'd love to have you back. Your first week back is on us.
> Want me to save you a spot? (Reply STOP if you'd rather not hear from us.)

### Step 4: Measure

List size per group → replies → came back for a class → rejoined → still paying after 3
months. We report it monthly.

## 6. Retention plan: keep the members we bring (and his existing ones)

Most people who quit a gym quit in the **first 90 days**, usually because they never built
the habit. Our plan focuses there.

### First 30 days: build the habit

| When | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Welcome WhatsApp: what to bring, how the screen and medals work, booking tips | Automated |
| After class 1 | "How was it?" plus an explanation of their first PXI score | Coach |
| Day 7 | "You've done X classes. Most members who stick do 3 a week" | Automated |
| Day 14 | Personal check-in, with a buddy intro if they come alone | Coach |
| Day 30 | First-month summary: classes, medals, progress. Celebrate it | Coach + content |

Goal: **8+ classes in the first month.** Members who get there usually stay.

### Every month: spot people slipping

- **No class in 10–14 days → a personal message from a coach.** Not a sales message: "Haven't
  seen you, all good?" This one habit saves the most members.
- **Before anyone cancels, offer a pause** (travel, injury, exams) instead of losing them.
  Ask why they're leaving and keep a record. It tells us what to fix.

### Keep it fun (and turn it into content)

- Milestones: 50 and 100 classes, first gold medal. A shout-out in the studio and on
  Instagram (with permission). Members love it, and it's free marketing content.
- Monthly PXI leaderboard, member of the month, the 8-week challenge, BFT Unlock.
- **Bring-a-friend days and a referral reward.** Friends of members join more often than any
  ad lead, and they stay longer because they have a buddy.

### Later idea (not now)

We're already building Coach Hub. Later, it could show coaches a "members you haven't seen
in 2 weeks" list. That's a natural add-on once Coach Hub is live and paid. Don't mention it
yet.

**Why we do this for his existing members too, even though we don't earn on them:** it
shows him value from month one, it's the strongest reason he'll renew with us, and the
same system protects our members.

## 7. What counts as "our" member (agree in writing)

**Ours:**

- Joined after contacting BFT through our ads, our tracked WhatsApp link or our lead form,
  within 60 days of that first contact
- Ex-members or old leads who rejoin within 60 days of our win-back messages
- Joined through a referral campaign we run

**Not ours:** walk-ins, his own referrals and anyone who didn't come through our links.

**Two clocks, two different jobs:**

| Clock | Question it answers | Starts | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **60 days** | *Is this member ours?* | The first time they contact BFT through our ad, link or message | 60 days to buy a membership |
| **12 months** | *How long do we get paid?* | Their first No Limits payment | 12 months (6 for win-backs) |

**Example: counts.** Sarah messages through our ad link on 1 Feb (60-day clock starts).
She buys the 2-week trial pass on 5 Feb (RM130, we get nothing). She joins No Limits weekly
on 20 Feb, day 19, so she's ours (12-month clock starts). Every weekly membership payment
(also RM130, which is easy to mix up with the trial) until 19 Feb 2028 gives us RM19.50. If she freezes for 4 weeks, we get nothing those weeks. If she cancels in
October, we stop there.

**Example: doesn't count.** Ahmad messages through our ad on 1 Feb, goes quiet, and joins on
15 May, more than 60 days later. Not ours. Too much else could have brought him back.

(The "first 30 days" in section 6 is only the welcome plan for new members. It has nothing
to do with payment.)

### How we track it

**The phone number links everything.** It's in the WhatsApp chat, the lead sheet and
Glofox.

| Step | What records it | How |
|---|---|---|
| **1. First contact** (60-day clock starts) | **Lead sheet** (shared Google Sheet) | Each ad's WhatsApp button sends a pre-filled message with a code, e.g. "Hi BFT! (ref FB-JAN)". Meta lead forms fill the sheet automatically. Win-backs: we have the list and the date we messaged each person |
| **2. Trial, then joins** (12-month clock starts) | **Glofox** | Sales team ticks "lead source" when signing them up. Glofox records the join date, plan and every payment |
| **3. Month end** | **Spreadsheet calculation** | Kelvin sends Glofox's monthly report of new members and payments. We match phone numbers against the lead sheet, keep only No Limits payments (no trials) inside each member's 12 months, and take 15% |
| **4. Invoice** | **Invoice + name list** | Every member we charge for is listed with first-contact date, join date and payments. Kelvin can check each one and question any |

**Where tracking can slip, and the fix:**

- *Sees our ad but walks in, calls or DMs on Instagram instead.* No code. Fix: the sales team
  asks every new person "How did you hear about us?" and records it. Agree in the contract
  what counts (e.g. "saw your ad" counts only if they're already on our lead sheet or name
  the ad).
- *Different phone number in WhatsApp and Glofox.* Match by name and email. A few each month
  at most.
- *Sales team forgets to log.* We fill the lead sheet automatically where we can (lead forms,
  WhatsApp codes), so little depends on them.

**What we need from Kelvin:**

1. Is BFT on the free WhatsApp Business app or the official WhatsApp Business platform? The
   platform shows automatically which ad each chat came from. The app needs the codes.
2. A monthly Glofox export of new members and payments, or a read-only staff login for
   reports.
3. A "lead source" field filled in for every new member in Glofox.
4. The sales team updates the lead sheet (trial booked, joined, not interested).

**Set-up:** about 2–3 days in December. Tracking codes on every ad, the lead sheet, the
automatic fill and the month-end calculation sheet. Test it on December's leads, so January
counts cleanly from day one.

**Since we replace his current agency in January,** all paid-ad leads will come through us.
That makes tracking clean, but it also means some of these members would have joined
anyway because of the brand and location. That's normal, and it's what he pays an agency
for today. Expect him to ask, so have the answer ready.
